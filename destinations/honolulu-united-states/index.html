Honolulu Travel Guide

Honolulu is a vibrant, tropical metropolis that serves as the gateway to the Hawaiian Islands, seamlessly blending ancient Polynesian heritage with modern American urban life. Nestled on the island of O'ahu, it is world-renowned for its iconic beaches, lush volcanic landscapes, and the warm, welcoming spirit of aloha.

About Honolulu

Honolulu, meaning 'sheltered harbor' in Hawaiian, has been a significant site for over a thousand years, originally settled by Polynesian voyagers. By the mid-19th century, it had evolved into the capital of the Hawaiian Kingdom, serving as a vital hub for trade and the seat of royal power, evidenced by the construction of the historic 'Iolani Palace.

The culture of Honolulu is a unique, harmonious fusion of Native Hawaiian traditions, Asian influences, and Western customs. This diversity is most vividly expressed in its culinary scene—featuring local staples like poke, loco moco, and shave ice—and its deep-rooted respect for the land and community, which remains central to daily life.

Quick facts

  • Honolulu is home to the only royal palace in the United States.
  • It is the most remote city of its size in the entire world.
  • The city is both the westernmost and the southernmost major city in the United States.
  • The name 'Honolulu' translates to 'sheltered bay' or 'protected harbor'.
  • Hawaii is the only U.S. state that was once an independent, internationally recognized kingdom.
