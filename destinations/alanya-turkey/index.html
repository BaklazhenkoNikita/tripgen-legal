Alanya Travel Guide

Alanya is a vibrant coastal gem on the Turkish Riviera, where sun-drenched Mediterranean beaches meet the rugged, dramatic backdrop of the Taurus Mountains. It seamlessly blends ancient history, with its iconic hilltop fortress, with a modern, lively atmosphere perfect for both relaxation and adventure.

About Alanya

Alanya’s strategic location has made it a coveted prize for centuries, serving as a notorious pirate hideout in ancient times before being brought under Roman control. Its golden age arrived in the 13th century under the Seljuk Sultan Alaaddin Keykubat I, who transformed the city into a vital naval base and his preferred winter capital, leaving behind the magnificent castle and shipyard that still define its skyline today. Following the Seljuk era, the city passed through the hands of the Karamanids and the Mamluks before being incorporated into the Ottoman Empire in 1471. Throughout these transitions, Alanya maintained its importance as a maritime hub, eventually evolving from a quiet coastal town into the world-renowned tourism destination it is recognized as today.

The culture of Alanya is a colorful tapestry woven from its deep-rooted Anatolian traditions and its status as a modern, international resort city. Locals take great pride in their hospitality, often welcoming visitors with traditional Turkish tea and a warm, relaxed pace of life that reflects the Mediterranean sun. The local food scene is a highlight, featuring fresh seafood, savory kebabs, and traditional pide, all often enjoyed in charming, historic settings near the harbor. Art and tradition are celebrated through vibrant festivals, such as the International Jazz Festival and various cultural events held at the base of the Red Tower. The city’s markets are a sensory delight, filled with the scent of local spices, handcrafted textiles, and the bustling energy of artisans continuing age-old crafts, ensuring that the spirit of the past remains very much alive in the daily rhythm of the city.

Quick facts
