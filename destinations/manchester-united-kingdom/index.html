Manchester Travel Guide

Often hailed as the world's first industrial city, Manchester is a vibrant, resilient metropolis that has reinvented itself as a global powerhouse of culture, innovation, and sport. From its rich industrial heritage to its cutting-edge music scene and world-famous football clubs, the city pulses with a unique, welcoming energy.

About Manchester

Manchester's history began with the Roman fort of Mamucium, established around AD 79, but it was the Industrial Revolution that truly defined the city. By the 19th century, it had become the global center of the cotton trade, earning it the nickname 'Cottonopolis' and driving massive urban expansion and technological advancement, including the world's first inter-city passenger railway. Following the decline of heavy industry in the 20th century, Manchester underwent a remarkable transformation. The city has since emerged as a modern cultural hub, bolstered by significant regeneration efforts—most notably following the 1996 city center bombing—which helped cement its status as the 'Capital of the North' and a leader in the arts, media, and technology.

Manchester’s culture is defined by its industrious spirit, famously symbolized by the worker bee, and a deep-rooted passion for music, football, and inclusivity. It is a city that thrives on creativity, boasting a legendary music scene that birthed iconic bands, a world-class array of museums and galleries, and a pioneering LGBTQ+ community centered around the vibrant Canal Street. The food scene is equally diverse and dynamic, ranging from the world-renowned 'Curry Mile' in Rusholme, packed with authentic South Asian eateries, to an ever-growing selection of high-end, Michelin-starred dining and trendy independent cafes in the Northern Quarter. The city's friendly, down-to-earth atmosphere makes it a place where history, innovation, and community spirit blend seamlessly.

Quick facts

  • Manchester has more miles of canals than Venice, a legacy of its industrial past.
  • It is the birthplace of the world's first professional football league, formed in 1888.
  • The world's first stored-program computer, nicknamed 'Baby,' was developed at the University of Manchester in 1948.
  • Chetham's Library, founded in 1653, is the oldest public library in the English-speaking world.
  • The city is home to a bronze statue of Abraham Lincoln, commemorating Manchester's historic solidarity with the U.S. against slavery during the American Civil War.
