Montevideo Travel Guide

Montevideo is a charismatic, laid-back capital that gracefully balances colonial history with a modern, coastal lifestyle along the Río de la Plata. Known for its vibrant arts scene and world-class waterfront, it offers a sophisticated yet authentic urban experience.

About Montevideo

Founded between 1724 and 1730 by Spanish governor Bruno Mauricio de Zabala, Montevideo was originally established as a strategic military garrison to counter Portuguese expansion from Brazil. The city evolved from a walled colonial outpost into a bustling port, playing a pivotal role in the struggle for Uruguayan independence in the early 19th century. Throughout the 19th and 20th centuries, the city navigated periods of political instability and foreign occupation, eventually emerging as a cultural and economic hub. Its architecture reflects this layered past, featuring a mix of colonial structures in the Ciudad Vieja and grand, eclectic buildings from the city's prosperous early 20th-century growth.

Montevideo's culture is deeply rooted in a blend of European, particularly Italian and Spanish, influences and a strong local identity. The city is famous for its _mate_ culture—you will see locals carrying thermoses and gourds everywhere—and its passionate love for football, which is practically a religion here. The food scene is a meat-lover's paradise, centered around the legendary Mercado del Puerto, where traditional _parrilladas_ (barbecues) are prepared over open wood fires. Culturally, the city hosts the world's longest carnival, a 40-day celebration filled with vibrant parades, rhythmic candombe drumming, and satirical murga performances.

Quick facts

  • Montevideo hosted every match of the very first FIFA World Cup in 1930.
  • The city's carnival is the longest in the world, lasting over 40 days.
  • La Rambla is one of the longest continuous sidewalks in the world, stretching over 22 kilometers.
  • The Palacio Salvo was the tallest building in South America when it was inaugurated in 1928.
  • The city's mausoleum for national hero José Gervasio Artigas is illuminated by a spotlight 24 hours a day.
