Toronto Travel Guide

Toronto is a vibrant, multicultural metropolis located on the northwestern shore of Lake Ontario, serving as Canada's financial, entertainment, and cultural heart. It is a dynamic city where iconic skyscrapers meet diverse, historic neighborhoods, offering a world-class blend of arts, cuisine, and urban energy.

About Toronto

Indigenous peoples have inhabited the Toronto area for over 10,000 years, with various nations utilizing the region's strategic waterways. In 1793, the British established the town of York, which served as the capital of Upper Canada; it was later incorporated and renamed Toronto in 1834 to distinguish it from other locations and shed its reputation as 'Muddy York.' Throughout the 19th and 20th centuries, Toronto evolved from a provincial town into a major industrial and financial powerhouse. Following the Second World War, the city experienced a massive influx of immigrants from around the globe, which fundamentally transformed its social fabric and established its identity as one of the most diverse and cosmopolitan cities in the world.

Toronto's culture is defined by its incredible diversity, with over half of its residents born outside of Canada and more than 170 languages spoken across the city. This 'mosaic' of cultures is best experienced through its distinct neighborhoods—such as Chinatown, Little Italy, and Kensington Market—which offer an authentic, global culinary scene that is a cornerstone of daily life. The city is also a major hub for the arts, hosting prestigious events like the Toronto International Film Festival (TIFF) and boasting a thriving theater, music, and museum scene. Whether exploring the historic Distillery District or catching a game at the Rogers Centre, Torontonians celebrate a blend of local traditions and international influences that make the city feel like a world within a city.

Quick facts

  • The CN Tower held the title of the world's tallest free-standing structure for 32 years until 2007.
  • Toronto is one of the most multicultural cities in the world, with over 50% of its population born outside of Canada.
  • The Toronto Islands, a popular recreational spot, were originally a peninsula until powerful storms in the mid-19th century separated them from the mainland.
  • The city's glass floor in the CN Tower is engineered to be strong enough to withstand the weight of 35 moose at once.
  • Toronto is often used as a filming location for major Hollywood productions, making it one of North America's top cities for movie and television production.
