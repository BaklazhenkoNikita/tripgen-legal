London Travel Guide

London is a sprawling, historic metropolis that seamlessly blends centuries of royal tradition with cutting-edge modern innovation. As a global hub for finance, arts, and culture, it offers an unparalleled experience where ancient landmarks stand alongside futuristic skyscrapers.

About London

Founded by the Romans as 'Londinium' around AD 43, London has evolved from a small trading settlement into one of the world's most influential cities. Throughout its long history, it has survived devastating events like the Great Plague of 1665 and the Great Fire of 1666, only to emerge stronger and more architecturally diverse each time. For centuries, London has been the political and cultural heart of the British Empire, serving as the seat of the monarchy and government. Its resilience during the Blitz of World War II remains a defining chapter in its modern identity, cementing its status as a symbol of endurance and global significance.

London is a vibrant, multicultural mosaic where over 300 languages are spoken, creating a dynamic atmosphere that influences everything from its world-class food scene to its thriving arts sector. The city is famous for its West End theatre district, historic pubs, and a deep-rooted love for both traditional customs and avant-garde creativity. Traditions like the Changing of the Guard coexist with a fast-paced, modern lifestyle. The culinary landscape is equally diverse, ranging from classic British staples like fish and chips and Sunday roasts to an incredible array of international cuisines reflecting the city's global population.

Quick facts

  • The London Underground, known as 'The Tube,' is the world's oldest rapid transit system, with its first line opening in 1863.
  • The Tower of London is home to six resident ravens; legend says that if they ever leave, the kingdom will fall.
  • London has over 170 museums, including the British Museum, which houses the Rosetta Stone.
  • The city's 'Big Ben' is actually the name of the great bell inside the Elizabeth Tower, not the tower itself.
  • London is home to four UNESCO World Heritage sites: the Tower of London, Maritime Greenwich, Westminster Palace, and Kew Gardens.
