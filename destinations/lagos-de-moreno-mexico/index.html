Lagos de Moreno Travel Guide

Lagos de Moreno is a charming, historic city in the Los Altos region of Jalisco, celebrated for its well-preserved colonial architecture and rich intellectual heritage. Often called the 'Athens of Jalisco,' it offers a blend of vibrant cultural traditions, stunning religious monuments, and a deep-rooted ranching history.

About Lagos de Moreno

Founded on March 31, 1563, as Villa de Santa María de los Lagos, the city served as a vital frontier settlement designed to protect the silver trade routes moving toward Zacatecas. Its strategic location along the historic Camino Real de Tierra Adentro—a UNESCO World Heritage route—cemented its status as a crucial commercial and agricultural hub during the colonial era. In the 19th century, the city became a focal point for the Mexican War of Independence, eventually being renamed in 1829 to honor the local insurgent hero Pedro Moreno. Throughout the 19th and early 20th centuries, the city flourished as a center for literature and the arts, earning its prestigious nickname and maintaining a legacy of intellectualism that persists today.

The culture of Lagos de Moreno is deeply tied to the traditions of the Los Altos region, characterized by a strong ranching identity, exquisite dairy production, and a profound sense of local pride. The city’s historic center, a designated Zone of Historical Monuments, is a living museum where colonial-era plazas and grand churches serve as the backdrop for daily life and lively community festivals. Gastronomy is a highlight here, with the local food scene featuring hearty regional specialties that reflect the area's agricultural roots. The arts remain central to the city's identity, with a long-standing tradition of poetry, theater, and music that continues to be celebrated in its historic venues and public spaces.

Quick facts

  • It is nicknamed the 'Athens of Jalisco' due to the high number of prominent writers, poets, and intellectuals born in the city.
  • The city is a key stop on the Camino Real de Tierra Adentro, a historic trade route recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage site.
  • It was founded in 1563 to protect silver shipments from the mines of Zacatecas from indigenous groups.
  • The city's historic center contains over 350 buildings of significant historical value, some dating back to the 17th century.
  • Despite its name ('Lagos' meaning lakes), the city is actually located on a high plateau in the Los Altos region.
