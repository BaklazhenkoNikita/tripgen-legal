Colombo Travel Guide

Colombo is the vibrant commercial capital of Sri Lanka, where colonial-era charm seamlessly blends with the fast-paced energy of a modernizing metropolis. It serves as the country's primary gateway, offering a sensory-rich experience defined by bustling street markets, serene temples, and a stunning oceanfront.

About Colombo

Colombo's strategic location on the Indian Ocean made it a coveted prize for colonial powers, with the Portuguese, Dutch, and British all leaving indelible marks on its architecture and urban layout. The city evolved from a small trading port into a major administrative hub, serving as the capital of the island under British rule from 1815 until the late 20th century. Following Sri Lanka's independence in 1948, Colombo continued to grow as the nation's economic heart. While the official administrative capital was moved to nearby Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte in the 1980s, Colombo remains the de facto center of commerce, finance, and culture, constantly reinventing itself while preserving its historical legacy in areas like the Fort district.

The culture of Colombo is a fascinating mosaic reflecting the island's diverse ethnic and religious heritage, where Sinhalese, Tamil, and Muslim influences coexist. This diversity is best experienced in the city's food scene, which ranges from high-end fusion restaurants to the legendary street food found in Pettah, such as _kottu roti_ and spicy short eats. Traditions are deeply rooted in Buddhist, Hindu, and Islamic practices, visible in the city's numerous temples and mosques that stand in close proximity. The arts scene is equally dynamic, with a growing number of galleries and theaters showcasing both traditional Kandyan dance and contemporary Sri Lankan creativity.

Quick facts

  • The city's name is believed to be derived from the classical Sinhalese name 'Kolon thota', meaning 'port on the river Kelani'.
  • The iconic Jami Ul-Alfar Mosque is often called the 'Red Mosque' because of its striking candy-cane-like red and white striped exterior.
  • Colombo is home to the Dutch Hospital, which is one of the oldest buildings in the area, dating back to the Dutch colonial era and now repurposed as a trendy shopping and dining precinct.
  • The city is known as the 'Green City of the East' due to its lush parks, such as Viharamahadevi Park, and its tree-lined boulevards.
  • Arthur C. Clarke, the famous science fiction author, lived in Colombo for many years and is said to have written parts of his later works while overlooking the ocean from the city.
