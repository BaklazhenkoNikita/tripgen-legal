Frankfurt am Main Travel Guide

Frankfurt is a dynamic global financial hub that seamlessly blends a futuristic, skyscraper-studded skyline with a charming, reconstructed medieval heart. Often called 'Mainhattan,' it offers a sophisticated mix of world-class museums, traditional apple wine taverns, and a bustling international atmosphere.

About Frankfurt am Main

First mentioned in 794 AD, Frankfurt rose to prominence as a vital center of the Holy Roman Empire, serving as the site for imperial elections and coronations for centuries. It enjoyed a long history as a Free Imperial City, fostering a strong tradition of trade and commerce that laid the foundation for its modern status as a financial powerhouse. While much of the city's historic Old Town was destroyed during World War II, Frankfurt has undergone extensive reconstruction, most notably in the Römerberg area. Today, it stands as a testament to resilience, balancing its deep historical roots with its role as the seat of the European Central Bank and a leader in global finance.

Frankfurt's culture is a fascinating study in contrasts, where high-powered banking professionals share the streets with students and artists. The city is famous for its 'Museumsufer' (Museum Embankment), a unique stretch along the Main River packed with diverse cultural institutions, and it hosts the world's largest book fair annually. Culinary life here is defined by the local 'Ebbelwoi' (apple wine) culture, best enjoyed in the traditional taverns of the Sachsenhausen district. Pair a glass of this tart, local specialty with 'Frankfurter Grüne Soße' (a herb-based green sauce) or a classic frankfurter sausage for an authentic taste of the city's heritage.

Quick facts

  • Frankfurt is the only city in Germany with a skyline of high-rise skyscrapers, earning it the nickname 'Mainhattan'.
  • The city is home to the Frankfurt Book Fair, which is the largest trade fair for books in the world by both the number of publishing companies represented and the number of visitors.
  • Frankfurt contains the largest city forest in Germany, covering a significant portion of the city's southern area.
  • The famous writer Johann Wolfgang von Goethe was born in Frankfurt, and his childhood home is now a popular museum.
  • Frankfurt is one of the few cities in the world that serves as the headquarters for a major central bank, the European Central Bank.
