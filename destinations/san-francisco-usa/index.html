San Francisco Travel Guide

San Francisco is a vibrant, hilly metropolis perched on the tip of a peninsula, renowned for its iconic Golden Gate Bridge and steep, fog-swept streets. It serves as a global hub for technology, progressive culture, and breathtaking coastal beauty.

About San Francisco

Founded in 1776 by Spanish colonists as a mission and presidio, the city exploded into prominence during the 1849 Gold Rush, transforming from a sleepy outpost into a bustling boomtown almost overnight. This rapid expansion set the stage for its character as a place of reinvention and opportunity. The city has endured significant trials, most notably the devastating 1906 earthquake and fire, which leveled much of the urban landscape. However, San Francisco’s resilience is legendary; it rebuilt quickly, eventually becoming a pivotal center for the 1960s counterculture movement and later, the global epicenter of the digital revolution in nearby Silicon Valley.

San Francisco’s culture is a melting pot of innovation, activism, and artistic expression, deeply rooted in its history of welcoming diverse communities. It is a city where _tech-forward thinking meets bohemian grit_, reflected in its eclectic neighborhoods, world-class culinary scene—ranging from Michelin-starred dining to legendary sourdough and dim sum—and a long-standing reputation as a sanctuary for LGBTQ+ rights and free speech. The arts scene is equally dynamic, featuring everything from historic Victorian architecture and world-class museums to vibrant street murals in the Mission District. Residents and visitors alike embrace an outdoor-oriented lifestyle, taking advantage of the city's proximity to stunning natural landscapes and a pervasive, laid-back West Coast attitude.

Quick facts

  • The Golden Gate Bridge is not actually 'gold' or 'red'; its official color is 'International Orange'.
  • San Francisco is built on more than 40 hills, which is why the city is famous for its steep streets and cable cars.
  • The fortune cookie was popularized in San Francisco, not China, with claims of its invention dating back to early 20th-century Japanese tea gardens in the city.
  • Alcatraz Island was the site of the first lighthouse built on the West Coast of the United States.
  • The city's famous fog has a name: locals often refer to it as 'Karl the Fog'.
