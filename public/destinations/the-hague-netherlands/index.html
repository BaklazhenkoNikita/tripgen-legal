The Hague Travel Guide

The Hague is the sophisticated administrative and political heart of the Netherlands, serving as the seat of government and the residence of the Dutch royal family. Renowned globally as the 'International City of Peace and Justice,' it seamlessly blends historic grandeur with a modern, coastal lifestyle along the North Sea.

About The Hague

The city's origins date back to 1248 when Count William II of Holland built a hunting lodge in a woodland area known as 'Haghe,' which eventually evolved into the Binnenhof, the center of Dutch political life. Over the centuries, it grew from a count's private enclosure into the de facto capital of the Dutch Republic, hosting the States-General and becoming a hub for European diplomacy. In the modern era, The Hague solidified its reputation as a global center for international law following the landmark Hague Conventions of 1899 and 1907. Today, it remains the permanent home to critical institutions such as the International Court of Justice and the International Criminal Court, cementing its role as a vital pillar of global governance.

The culture of The Hague is a refined mix of international diplomacy and traditional Dutch charm, characterized by a diverse, multilingual population and an elegant, understated atmosphere. The city boasts a world-class art scene, anchored by the Mauritshuis, which holds masterpieces from the Dutch Golden Age, and the whimsical, mind-bending works of M.C. Escher. The local food scene reflects its international character, offering everything from traditional Dutch snacks like _haring_ (raw herring) and _stroopwafels_ to high-end global cuisine. Residents often enjoy a relaxed lifestyle, cycling between the historic city center and the vibrant seaside district of Scheveningen, where the beach boardwalk provides a lively contrast to the quiet, stately government quarters.

Quick facts

  • Although Amsterdam is the official capital of the Netherlands, The Hague has been the seat of government and the residence of the monarchy for centuries.
  • The city's name originates from 'des Graven hage,' which translates to 'The Count's Hedge' or 'The Count's Hunting Grounds.'
  • The Panorama Mesdag is the oldest 19th-century panoramic painting in the world still in its original location, offering a 360-degree view of the sea and dunes as they appeared in 1881.
  • The Hague is home to the International Court of Justice, which is the only principal organ of the United Nations located outside of New York City.
  • The city is famously known as 'The Residence' (De Residentie) because it has served as the home of the Dutch royal family since the 16th century.
