Hiroshima Travel Guide

Hiroshima is a resilient and vibrant city that has transformed from a site of profound tragedy into a global symbol of peace and hope. Nestled between the Seto Inland Sea and the Chugoku Mountains, it offers a poignant blend of somber historical reflection and modern, scenic beauty.

About Hiroshima

Founded in the 16th century as a castle town, Hiroshima grew into a significant industrial and military hub by the early 20th century. Its history was irrevocably altered on August 6, 1945, when it became the first city in the world to be targeted by an atomic bomb, resulting in catastrophic destruction and loss of life. In the decades following the war, Hiroshima underwent a remarkable reconstruction, rising from the ashes to become a thriving metropolis. Today, the city is internationally recognized for its dedicated commitment to nuclear disarmament and its enduring message of world peace, embodied by the preserved ruins of the Atomic Bomb Dome.

Hiroshima’s culture is defined by a spirit of resilience and a deep-seated dedication to peace, which permeates its public life and annual ceremonies. The city maintains a welcoming, laid-back atmosphere that balances its solemn historical sites with a lively, modern urban energy. The local food scene is a major highlight, most famously for its unique style of **okonomiyaki**—a savory, layered pancake cooked on a hot griddle—and its exceptionally plump, fresh oysters harvested from the nearby Seto Inland Sea. The region also boasts a rich artistic heritage, with numerous museums and a vibrant community that celebrates both traditional Japanese crafts and contemporary expression.

Quick facts

  • The Atomic Bomb Dome was originally the Hiroshima Prefectural Industrial Promotion Hall, designed by a Czech architect, and its survival is considered a miracle given its proximity to the hypocenter.
  • Miyajima Island is famously known as 'God's Island,' and its iconic Otorii gate appears to float on the water during high tide.
  • Hiroshima Castle, often called the 'Carp Castle,' was completely destroyed in 1945 and meticulously reconstructed in the 1950s as a symbol of the city's rebirth.
  • Okonomimura, or 'Okonomiyaki Village,' is a dedicated building in the city center containing over 20 different restaurants all serving the local specialty.
  • Okunoshima, a small island near Hiroshima, is famously known as 'Rabbit Island' because it is populated by hundreds of friendly, wild rabbits.
