Cabo San Lucas Travel Guide

Cabo San Lucas is a sun-drenched paradise located at the southern tip of the Baja California Peninsula, where the rugged desert landscape dramatically meets the turquoise waters of the Sea of Cortez. Renowned for its iconic rock formations, world-class sport fishing, and vibrant nightlife, it serves as a premier destination for luxury seekers and adventure enthusiasts alike.

About Cabo San Lucas

The region has been inhabited for over 10,000 years, originally by the indigenous Pericú people who thrived as hunter-gatherers. Following the arrival of Spanish explorers like Hernán Cortés in the 16th century, the area became a strategic, albeit remote, stop for trade ships and a notorious hideout for pirates who preyed on passing vessels. For centuries, it remained a sleepy fishing village until the mid-20th century, when it began attracting Hollywood elite seeking seclusion. The completion of the Transpeninsular Highway in the 1970s transformed the area into a major international tourist hub, evolving from a quiet maritime outpost into the bustling, high-end resort destination it is today.

Cabo’s culture is a vibrant fusion of traditional Mexican heritage and a modern, international resort lifestyle. The local food scene is a highlight, celebrated for its incredibly fresh seafood—especially fish tacos and ceviche—often enjoyed at lively, open-air beach clubs or high-end restaurants helmed by world-renowned chefs. The town pulses with a high-energy atmosphere, particularly in its nightlife, yet it maintains a connection to its roots through local artisan markets and cultural festivals. Visitors often find a welcoming, laid-back vibe where the _mañana_ spirit blends seamlessly with the excitement of world-class water sports and championship golf.

Quick facts

  • The Sea of Cortez is often called 'The Aquarium of the World' by oceanographers due to its immense marine biodiversity.
  • Cabo is known as the 'Striped Marlin Capital of the World' and hosts the world's richest fishing tournament, the Bisbee's Black & Blue.
  • The famous 'Lovers Beach' at Land's End is only accessible by boat and sits where the Pacific Ocean meets the Sea of Cortez.
  • The Sea of Cortez is geologically very young, having formed only about three million years ago.
  • Underwater 'sandfalls'—cascading rivers of sand flowing down into deep submarine canyons—were famously discovered here by Jacques Cousteau.
