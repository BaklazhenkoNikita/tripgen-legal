Siem Reap Travel Guide

Siem Reap is the vibrant gateway to the awe-inspiring Angkor Archaeological Park, home to the world's largest religious monument, Angkor Wat. Beyond its ancient wonders, the city offers a captivating blend of French-colonial charm, bustling night markets, and a rapidly evolving culinary scene.

About Siem Reap

The region served as the heart of the Khmer Empire from the 9th to the 15th century, with kings constructing massive temple complexes and an intricate network of water management systems. After centuries of relative obscurity, the area gained international attention in the 19th century when French explorers 'rediscovered' the ruins, leading to extensive restoration efforts by the École française d'Extrême-Orient starting in 1901. In the 20th century, the city endured the tragic upheaval of the Khmer Rouge regime, which devastated the country's infrastructure and population. Since the late 1990s, Siem Reap has experienced a remarkable resurgence, transforming from a quiet provincial town into a global tourism hub that honors its profound historical legacy while embracing modern development.

Siem Reap's culture is deeply rooted in Khmer traditions, visible in the graceful movements of Apsara dance performances and the meticulous craftsmanship of local artisans who specialize in silk weaving, stone carving, and lacquerware. The city is a melting pot where ancient spiritual practices coexist with a cosmopolitan atmosphere, featuring a lively nightlife centered around Pub Street and a food scene that highlights fresh, herb-forward Khmer cuisine like fish amok. Community and heritage preservation are central to the local identity, with many social enterprises working to train the next generation of craftspeople. Visitors often find a welcoming, resilient spirit among the locals, who take great pride in sharing the history of the Khmer Empire with the world.

Quick facts

  • The name 'Siem Reap' literally translates to 'Defeat of Siam,' commemorating a historic victory of the Khmer Empire over the Thai kingdom.
  • Angkor Wat is the largest religious structure in the world and is so iconic that it is featured on the national flag of Cambodia.
  • The Siem Reap River is not entirely natural; it was artificially engineered by ancient Khmer kings to provide water to the city.
  • Ta Prohm is famous for being left largely as it was found, with massive silk-cotton and strangler fig trees growing directly out of the temple ruins.
  • The city was designated as the ASEAN City of Culture for 2021–2022.
