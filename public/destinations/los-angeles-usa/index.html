Los Angeles Travel Guide

Known as the 'City of Angels,' Los Angeles is a sprawling, sun-drenched metropolis that serves as the global epicenter of the entertainment industry. It is a vibrant tapestry of diverse neighborhoods, iconic landmarks, and a creative spirit that defines modern American culture.

About Los Angeles

Originally inhabited by the Tongva people, the area was settled by Spanish colonists in 1781 as 'El Pueblo de Nuestra Señora la Reina de los Ángeles.' After becoming part of Mexico and eventually the United States in 1848, the city experienced explosive growth in the early 20th century, fueled by the discovery of oil and the rise of the nascent film industry in Hollywood. By the 1920s, it had firmly established itself as the world's entertainment capital, a title it continues to hold today.

Los Angeles is a true melting pot, home to residents from over 140 countries who speak more than 200 languages, resulting in an incredibly diverse culinary scene ranging from authentic street tacos to world-class fine dining. The city's culture is deeply rooted in creativity, boasting more museums and art galleries than any other city in the world, alongside a fast-paced lifestyle that balances urban ambition with a laid-back, beach-oriented coastal sensibility.

Quick facts

  • Los Angeles is home to more museums and art galleries than any other city in the world.
  • There are at least 11 miles of hidden, Prohibition-era tunnels beneath downtown Los Angeles that were once used to transport people and illicit goods.
  • It is one of the few places on Earth where you can theoretically ski in the mountains and surf in the ocean on the same day.
  • The famous Hollywood Sign was originally erected in 1923 as a real estate advertisement for a development called 'Hollywoodland.'
  • Beverly Hills, now synonymous with luxury, was once a ranch primarily used for growing lima beans.
