Tangier Travel Guide

Poised at the northern tip of Morocco where the Mediterranean Sea meets the Atlantic Ocean, Tangier is a legendary, cosmopolitan gateway between Africa and Europe. This "Bride of the North" enchants visitors with its labyrinthine medina, dazzling white architecture, and a rich, bohemian history that has long attracted artists and writers.

About Tangier

Tangier is one of North Africa's oldest cities, with roots stretching back to Phoenician trading posts in the 10th century BCE. Throughout its long history, it has been a coveted strategic prize, falling under the control of Carthaginians, Romans, Arabs, Portuguese, Spanish, and British forces before finally returning to Moroccan sovereignty in 1956. For much of the early 20th century, Tangier existed as an 'International Zone,' a unique status that turned it into a magnet for spies, diplomats, and eccentric expatriates. This period of international administration fostered a climate of intense political and artistic ferment, cementing the city's reputation as a sanctuary for the unconventional and the creative.

The culture of Tangier is a vibrant, sensory-rich tapestry woven from Berber, Arab, Andalusian, and European influences. The city is famous for its laid-back cafe culture, where locals and travelers alike gather to sip mint tea and engage in the art of conversation, a tradition that has persisted since the days when literary icons like Paul Bowles and William S. Burroughs roamed its streets. The food scene is a delicious reflection of its geography, featuring fresh Mediterranean seafood, traditional Moroccan tagines, and Spanish-influenced tapas. The arts remain a cornerstone of Tangier's identity, with the city currently experiencing a creative renaissance as a new generation of designers, filmmakers, and musicians flock to its historic, sun-drenched alleyways.

Quick facts

  • Tangier was the site of the first piece of foreign property ever owned by the United States government, gifted by the Sultan of Morocco in 1821.
  • The sea opening of the Hercules Cave is shaped remarkably like the continent of Africa, a feature often referred to as 'The Map of Africa.'
  • During its time as an International Zone, Tangier became a legendary haven for beatnik writers and artists, including Jack Kerouac and Henri Matisse.
  • The city is often called the 'White City' due to its iconic, whitewashed buildings that reflect the intense North African sunlight.
  • Tangier is located just 17 miles (27 km) across the Strait of Gibraltar from Spain, making it one of the closest points between the two continents.
