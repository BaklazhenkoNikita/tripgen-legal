Salzburg Travel Guide

Nestled at the foot of the Eastern Alps, Salzburg is a breathtaking city renowned for its well-preserved Baroque architecture and its status as the birthplace of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart. It seamlessly blends a rich musical heritage with stunning alpine scenery, making it one of Europe's most enchanting cultural destinations.

About Salzburg

Salzburg's roots stretch back to the Roman settlement of Iuvavum, but it truly flourished after being established as an episcopal see in 696 AD. For centuries, the city was ruled by powerful prince-archbishops who grew wealthy from the local salt trade—often called 'white gold'—which allowed them to commission the magnificent churches and palaces that define the city's skyline today. Throughout the Middle Ages and the Renaissance, Salzburg served as a vital ecclesiastical center and a bastion of the Counter-Reformation. It was not until 1816, following the Napoleonic Wars and the Congress of Vienna, that Salzburg was finally incorporated into the Austrian Empire, transitioning from an independent prince-bishopric to a key cultural jewel of modern Austria.

Culture in Salzburg is inextricably linked to classical music, most notably through the world-famous Salzburg Festival, which attracts elite performers and audiences annually. The city's atmosphere is a sophisticated mix of traditional Austrian hospitality, Catholic heritage, and a vibrant student life, all set against a backdrop of historic coffee houses and bustling beer gardens. The local food scene is a delicious reflection of alpine traditions, featuring hearty dishes like Wiener Schnitzel and Knödel, often followed by the iconic Salzburger Nockerl, a fluffy, soufflé-like dessert. _Insider tip: Don't leave without trying a genuine Mozartkugel, the pistachio-marzipan-nougat confection that has become the city's unofficial sweet emblem._

Quick facts

  • The city's name literally translates to 'Salt Fortress,' referencing the barges carrying salt on the Salzach River that were once taxed by the local archbishops.
  • Salzburg is the setting for the iconic film 'The Sound of Music,' and many visitors still take guided tours to see the real-life locations used in the movie.
  • Despite its global fame, Salzburg was only incorporated into Austria in 1816; before that, it was an independent state ruled by prince-archbishops for over 600 years.
  • The Hohensalzburg Fortress is one of the largest and best-preserved medieval castles in Europe, and remarkably, it has never been captured by an enemy army.
  • The city's historic center is a UNESCO World Heritage site, celebrated for having one of the most intact Baroque cityscapes north of the Alps.
