Valencia Travel Guide

Valencia is a vibrant Mediterranean city that masterfully balances its deep-rooted medieval history with breathtaking futuristic architecture. It is a place where golden beaches meet lush urban parks, all centered around a world-class culinary scene that famously gave birth to paella.

About Valencia

Founded as a Roman colony in 138 BC by veteran soldiers, the city was named 'Valentia,' meaning 'bravery' or 'courage.' Throughout its long history, it has been shaped by Visigothic, Moorish, and Christian influences, most notably falling under the rule of the legendary El Cid in the 11th century before being conquered by James I of Aragon in 1238. During the 15th century, Valencia experienced a 'Golden Age' as a major Mediterranean commercial hub, evidenced by the construction of the iconic Silk Exchange. In more recent history, the city served as the provisional capital of Spain during the Spanish Civil War (1936–1939) and underwent a massive urban transformation in the late 20th century, most notably diverting the flood-prone Turia River to create the expansive green belt that defines the city today.

Valencian culture is a spirited blend of Mediterranean tradition and modern innovation, deeply proud of its distinct Valencian language and heritage. The city is perhaps best known for the _Fallas_ festival, a spectacular annual event where massive, intricate satirical sculptures are paraded through the streets before being ceremoniously burned in a blaze of fireworks and community celebration. The food scene is the heart of local life, centered around the iconic _paella Valenciana_—traditionally made with chicken, rabbit, and snails—which locals insist must be eaten at lunch. Beyond the plate, the city maintains ancient traditions like the Water Court (_Tribunal de las Aguas_), a medieval institution that still meets weekly at the cathedral to resolve irrigation disputes, proving that in Valencia, the past is never truly left behind.

Quick facts

  • The city is home to the 'Holy Chalice,' which many believe to be the authentic cup used by Jesus at the Last Supper.
  • The Turia Gardens, one of Spain's largest urban parks, was created in the former riverbed of the Turia River after it was diverted to prevent flooding.
  • Valencia is the birthplace of paella, and the traditional recipe is strictly protected by local culinary standards.
  • The city contains the narrowest building in Europe, known as 'La Estrecha,' which measures only 107 centimeters wide.
  • Valencia was the provisional capital of Spain during the Spanish Civil War.
