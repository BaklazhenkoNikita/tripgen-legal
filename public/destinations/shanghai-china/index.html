Shanghai Travel Guide

Shanghai is a dazzling, high-energy metropolis where futuristic skyscrapers stand in stark, beautiful contrast to historic colonial architecture. As China's premier financial and cultural hub, it offers a unique blend of traditional Chinese heritage and cosmopolitan, international flair.

About Shanghai

Once a modest fishing village, Shanghai rose to prominence in the 19th century after the First Opium War, when it was opened as a treaty port. This era saw the city divided into foreign concessions—British, French, and American—which fostered a unique, multicultural environment and a rapid economic boom that earned it the nickname 'Paris of the East' by the 1930s. Following the founding of the People's Republic of China, the city underwent significant shifts, but it truly transformed into the global powerhouse it is today after the 1978 economic reforms. The development of the Pudong district in the 1990s turned a stretch of farmland into one of the world's most iconic skylines, cementing Shanghai's status as a modern, world-class financial center.

Shanghai's culture is defined by 'Haipai,' a unique fusion of traditional Chinese customs and Western influences that emerged during the colonial era. This openness to the world is reflected in everything from the city's sophisticated fashion and art scene to its distinct, slightly sweet culinary style, famously exemplified by the delicate _xiaolongbao_ (soup dumplings). Life in Shanghai is a fast-paced mix of old and new; you might find elderly residents practicing tai chi in a park at dawn, while just a few blocks away, young professionals navigate the cutting-edge digital economy. The city also boasts a vibrant 'coffee culture' and a deep appreciation for history, preserved in the charming Shikumen stone-gate houses and the quiet, leafy streets of the former French Concession.

Quick facts

  • Shanghai is home to the world's longest metro system, spanning hundreds of kilometers of track.
  • The city is often called the 'Magic City' due to its rapid, almost supernatural transformation over the last few decades.
  • The Shanghai Maglev is the world's fastest commercial electric train, reaching speeds of up to 431 km/h.
  • Local Shanghainese dialect is distinct from Mandarin and is often unintelligible to speakers of standard Chinese.
  • People's Park hosts a famous 'Marriage Market' where parents post resumes of their unmarried children to find potential matches.
