Oaxaca Travel Guide

Oaxaca is widely considered the cultural and gastronomic heart of Mexico, celebrated for its profound indigenous roots, vibrant colonial architecture, and world-renowned culinary traditions. It is a place where ancient Zapotec and Mixtec legacies seamlessly blend with Spanish colonial history to create a uniquely soulful atmosphere.

About Oaxaca

The Oaxaca Valley has been inhabited for over 11,000 years, serving as the cradle for sophisticated civilizations like the Zapotecs, who established the magnificent hilltop city of Monte Albán around 500 B.C. Following the decline of these early powers, the region saw Mixtec influence and eventually Aztec control before the Spanish arrived in the 16th century to establish the colonial city that stands today. Throughout the centuries, Oaxaca has remained a bastion of indigenous identity, largely due to its rugged, isolating mountain terrain. It played a pivotal role in Mexican history as the birthplace of Benito Juárez, the country's first indigenous president, and has consistently served as a center for political and cultural resistance and preservation.

Oaxacan culture is a living tapestry of traditions, most notably seen in its deep-rooted artisan communities that produce everything from intricate alebrijes (wood carvings) to world-famous black clay pottery and vibrant textiles. The city is a sensory explosion, famous for its complex moles, artisanal mezcal, and bustling markets where the scent of roasted chilies and chocolate fills the air. Life here revolves around the Zócalo, a lively central plaza where locals and visitors gather to enjoy music, coffee, and conversation. The city is also renowned for its spectacular festivals, particularly the Day of the Dead (Día de los Muertos), which is celebrated with a level of devotion and artistic flair that is unmatched anywhere else in the world.

Quick facts

  • Oaxaca is widely recognized as the birthplace of corn and squash, which were domesticated in the region thousands of years ago.
  • The state of Oaxaca is the most ethnically and linguistically diverse in Mexico, home to 16 officially recognized indigenous groups.
  • It is the global capital of mezcal, an artisanal spirit distilled from agave that is deeply tied to local community identity and ritual.
  • The city's historic center and the nearby ruins of Monte Albán are both designated UNESCO World Heritage sites.
  • The famous 'El Tule' tree, located just outside the city, is one of the oldest and widest living trees in the world, estimated to be over 2,000 years old.
