Puerto Vallarta Travel Guide

Puerto Vallarta is a vibrant coastal gem where the lush Sierra Madre mountains dramatically meet the sparkling waters of Banderas Bay. It perfectly balances authentic Mexican charm with world-class resort luxury, offering everything from cobblestone streets and traditional festivals to stunning beaches and a thriving culinary scene.

About Puerto Vallarta

Originally a small fishing and farming village known as Las Peñas, the town was officially founded in 1851 by Guadalupe Sánchez and his family. For decades, it remained a quiet, secluded enclave until it was thrust into the international spotlight in the 1960s. The filming of the movie 'The Night of the Iguana' brought Hollywood stars Elizabeth Taylor and Richard Burton to the area, sparking a legendary romance that transformed the sleepy town into a world-renowned tourist destination almost overnight. Since that cinematic boom, Puerto Vallarta has evolved into a sophisticated metropolitan area while carefully preserving its historic core. It has successfully maintained its 'Mexican soul' through the protection of its colonial architecture and traditional customs, even as it expanded to include modern marinas, luxury resorts, and a bustling international cruise port.

The cultural heartbeat of Puerto Vallarta is found in its historic center, where the iconic Our Lady of Guadalupe Parish stands as a testament to the city's deep spiritual roots. The city is a haven for art lovers, boasting a vibrant gallery scene and a famous Malecón boardwalk adorned with open-air sculptures that celebrate local and international creativity. The local food scene is equally impressive, ranging from humble, authentic taco stands to high-end restaurants that masterfully blend traditional Mexican flavors with modern culinary techniques. Traditions are cherished here, with major celebrations like the Day of the Dead and the Patron Saint Festivities for the Virgin of Guadalupe bringing the community together in colorful, heartfelt displays. Visitors are encouraged to explore the local markets, where artisans sell handmade ceramics, textiles, and jewelry, offering a direct connection to the region's rich heritage and craftsmanship.

Quick facts

  • The city was originally named Las Peñas before being renamed in 1918 to honor Ignacio L. Vallarta, a former governor of Jalisco.
  • The intense media coverage of Elizabeth Taylor and Richard Burton's love affair in the 1960s is widely credited with putting Puerto Vallarta on the global tourism map.
  • Banderas Bay is one of the largest natural bays in the world and serves as a critical breeding ground for humpback whales during the winter months.
  • The city is home to a unique 'hidden beach' located inside a crater on the Marietas Islands, which can only be reached by swimming through a tunnel.
  • Puerto Vallarta is one of the few places in Mexico where you can find a blend of traditional colonial architecture and modern, high-end resort amenities seamlessly integrated into the same landscape.
