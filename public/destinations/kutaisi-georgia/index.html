Kutaisi Travel Guide

Kutaisi is one of the world's oldest continuously inhabited cities, serving as a captivating bridge between Georgia's ancient mythological past and its vibrant modern identity. Nestled along the banks of the Rioni River, it offers a relaxed, authentic atmosphere filled with medieval architecture, lush landscapes, and legendary hospitality.

About Kutaisi

With roots stretching back to the 13th century BC, Kutaisi served as the legendary capital of the Kingdom of Colchis, the destination of Jason and the Argonauts in their quest for the Golden Fleece. Throughout the Middle Ages, it functioned as the political and cultural heart of Georgia, acting as the capital of the united Georgian monarchy from the 8th to the 11th century. Over the centuries, the city has weathered numerous invasions and shifts in power, yet it has remained a resilient center of Georgian intellect and spirituality. Its historical significance is anchored by UNESCO-listed sites that showcase the pinnacle of medieval Georgian architecture, reflecting a legacy that has survived empires and shaped the nation's soul.

The culture of Kutaisi is defined by the warmth of Imeretian hospitality, where food and family are at the center of life. The local culinary scene is legendary, featuring regional specialties like Imeruli khachapuri (cheese-filled bread), pkhali (vegetable pâté), and a variety of dishes seasoned with walnuts and local herbs. Sharing a meal here is a sacred tradition, often accompanied by polyphonic singing and the spirited toasts that define Georgian social gatherings. Art and tradition thrive in the city's daily life, from the bustling, colorful local markets to the performances at the historic drama and opera theaters. Residents take immense pride in their heritage, with folk dance and music being integral parts of education and community celebrations, ensuring that the ancient spirit of the region remains vibrant and alive.

Quick facts

  • Kutaisi is considered one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, with a history spanning over 3,000 years.
  • The Colchis Fountain in the city center features enlarged, gilded replicas of Bronze Age artifacts discovered in the region, connecting modern residents to their ancient ancestors.
  • The nearby Sataplia Nature Reserve contains actual fossilized dinosaur footprints, offering a prehistoric glimpse into the region's deep geological past.
  • The city's White Bridge features transparent glass panels, allowing pedestrians to look directly down at the rushing waters of the Rioni River beneath their feet.
  • Kutaisi was the site of a bold architectural experiment when the Georgian Parliament was moved to a futuristic, glass-domed building in the city in 2012, before later returning to the capital.
