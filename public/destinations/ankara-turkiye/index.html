Ankara Travel Guide

Ankara is the vibrant, administrative heart of Türkiye, serving as a modern capital that balances its role as a political center with a deep, layered history. It is a city where grand monuments to the nation's founding stand alongside ancient ruins, reflecting a unique blend of Turkish heritage and contemporary urban life.

About Ankara

Ankara's history stretches back thousands of years, with early settlements dating to the Hittite and Phrygian periods. Throughout antiquity, it served as a strategic hub for various civilizations, including the Galatians, Romans, and Byzantines, before eventually becoming an important Ottoman administrative center. In the 20th century, the city's significance was transformed when Mustafa Kemal Atatürk chose it as the headquarters for the Turkish National Movement during the War of Independence. Following the collapse of the Ottoman Empire, Ankara was officially declared the capital of the new Republic of Türkiye in 1923, evolving rapidly from a small town into the sprawling, modern metropolis it is today.

The culture of Ankara is a sophisticated mix of traditional Anatolian values and the cosmopolitan energy of a capital city. As the seat of government and home to numerous universities, the city boasts a lively intellectual scene, with a wealth of museums, theaters, and concert halls that celebrate both Turkish and international arts. The local food scene is a highlight, featuring hearty Central Anatolian cuisine, including famous regional specialties like Ankara tava (a lamb and orzo dish) and high-quality local honey and grapes. The city's social life revolves around its bustling markets, modern cafes, and the historic streets surrounding the citadel, where traditional crafts and modern commerce coexist seamlessly.

Quick facts

  • Ankara was historically known as 'Angora' and gave its name to the Angora rabbit, Angora goat (source of mohair), and the Angora cat.
  • The Temple of Augustus contains the 'Monumentum Ancyranum,' an inscription of the 'Res Gestae Divi Augusti,' which is the most complete surviving record of the life and achievements of the first Roman Emperor, Augustus.
  • Despite being a major metropolis today, in 1923 when it was named the capital, it was a relatively small town with a population of only about 35,000 people.
  • The city is situated on a high, dry plateau, which gives it a distinct continental climate that is significantly different from the coastal Mediterranean regions of Türkiye.
  • Ankara is home to the largest number of foreign embassies in Türkiye, cementing its status as the nation's diplomatic hub.
