Turku Travel Guide

As Finland's oldest city and former capital, Turku is a vibrant blend of medieval history and modern urban charm situated along the picturesque Aura River. Often called the 'Paris of Finland,' it offers a sophisticated culinary scene, rich cultural heritage, and easy access to the stunning Finnish archipelago.

About Turku

Founded in the 13th century, Turku served as the most important city in Finland for centuries, acting as the administrative and religious center under both Swedish and Russian rule. It was the site of Finland's first university, the Royal Academy of Turku, established in 1640, and remained the national capital until 1812 when the status was transferred to Helsinki. The city's landscape was dramatically altered by the Great Fire of 1827, which destroyed most of the wooden buildings in the center. While the city was rebuilt according to a new grid plan, pockets of its medieval past, such as the iconic Turku Castle and the 13th-century Cathedral, survived to tell the story of its long and influential history.

Turku boasts a unique, relaxed culture defined by its 'riverbank life,' where locals and visitors alike gather at riverside cafés, restaurants, and moored boats. The city is officially recognized as the 'Food Capital of Finland,' celebrated for its high-quality dining scene that emphasizes local, seasonal ingredients and a long tradition of culinary excellence. Art and music are deeply woven into the city's identity, with numerous museums, galleries, and festivals held throughout the year. The city is also a bilingual hub, reflecting its historical ties to Sweden, and maintains a strong connection to its maritime roots, which are celebrated through its active port and proximity to the vast, island-dotted archipelago.

Quick facts

  • Turku is the oldest city in Finland, with records dating back to 1229.
  • The city is officially declared the 'Christmas City' of Finland and hosts the traditional 'Declaration of Christmas Peace' every year.
  • Locals divide the city into two sides based on the river: 'täl pual jokke' (this side of the river) and 'tois pual jokke' (the other side).
  • Turku was the European Capital of Culture in 2011.
  • The city is home to the only medieval castle in Finland that is still open to the public.
