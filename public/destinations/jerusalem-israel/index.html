Jerusalem Travel Guide

Jerusalem is a profound, ancient city that serves as the spiritual epicenter for Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. It is a place where thousands of years of history collide with a vibrant, modern culture, creating an atmosphere unlike anywhere else on Earth.

About Jerusalem

With a history spanning over 5,000 years, Jerusalem has been a focal point of human civilization, having been besieged, captured, and destroyed dozens of times. It was established as the capital of the United Kingdom of Israel by King David around 1000 BCE, and has since been ruled by various empires, including the Babylonians, Romans, Crusaders, and the Ottoman Turks, who built the iconic walls that still define the Old City today. In the modern era, the city was divided following the 1948 Arab-Israeli War, with Israel controlling the western portion and Jordan the east. Following the 1967 Six-Day War, Israel gained control of the entire city, and it remains the seat of the Israeli government, though its status continues to be a central and deeply sensitive issue in the broader Israeli-Palestinian conflict.

The culture of Jerusalem is a complex tapestry woven from the diverse traditions of its Jewish, Muslim, Christian, and Armenian communities. Daily life is often governed by the rhythms of religious observance, yet the city also boasts a thriving secular side, featuring world-class museums, a booming high-tech scene, and a culinary landscape that blends Middle Eastern staples like hummus and falafel with innovative, modern Israeli fusion cuisine. Visitors will find a city of stark contrasts, where one can walk from the solemn, ancient atmosphere of the Old City's narrow alleyways into the bustling, trendy streets of West Jerusalem filled with hipster cafes and lively markets. The city's calendar is packed with festivals, from the atmospheric Light Festival in the Old City to the vibrant, sensory-overload experience of the Mahane Yehuda Market.

Quick facts

  • The Mount of Olives is considered the oldest active burial site in the world, with over 150,000 graves.
  • Jerusalem has been conquered or recaptured more than 40 times throughout its long history.
  • The city contains more than 2,000 active archaeological sites.
  • The name 'Jerusalem' is mentioned over 900 times in the Hebrew Bible.
