Granada Travel Guide

Granada is a breathtaking Andalusian city nestled at the foot of the Sierra Nevada mountains, renowned as the final stronghold of Moorish Spain. It is a place where centuries of Islamic history, vibrant flamenco culture, and a legendary tapas scene converge in a stunning, sun-drenched landscape.

About Granada

Granada's history is a rich tapestry woven from Iberian, Roman, and Visigothic roots, but it truly flourished as the capital of the Nasrid dynasty, the last Muslim-ruled state in the Iberian Peninsula. For over 700 years, the city served as a beacon of Islamic art and science, culminating in the construction of the iconic Alhambra palace complex. In 1492, the city fell to the Catholic Monarchs, Ferdinand and Isabella, marking the end of the Reconquista. This pivotal moment transformed Granada into a Christian city, leading to the construction of its grand cathedral and the burial of the monarchs in the Royal Chapel, forever blending Islamic architectural heritage with Renaissance and Baroque influences.

The culture of Granada is deeply rooted in its Andalusian identity, characterized by a passionate, laid-back lifestyle and a unique sense of humor known as 'malafollá'. The city is famous for its vibrant flamenco scene, often performed in the intimate, cave-like dwellings of the Sacromonte district, where the art form feels raw and deeply personal. The food scene is legendary, particularly for its generous tradition of serving free, substantial tapas with every drink ordered. Locals and students alike spend their evenings hopping between lively bars, enjoying local specialties like 'habas con jamón' (fava beans with ham) and 'tortilla del Sacromonte', all while soaking in the city's historic, narrow-street atmosphere.

Quick facts

  • Granada is one of the few places in the world where you can potentially ski in the Sierra Nevada mountains and swim in the Mediterranean Sea on the same day.
  • The city is famous for its 'free tapas' culture, where ordering a drink almost always guarantees a complimentary plate of food.
  • The pomegranate is the city's symbol and appears on its coat of arms; the name 'Granada' is also the Spanish word for the fruit.
  • In 1330, Granada was the most populated city in Europe, serving as a major center of trade and culture.
  • The Alhambra is the most visited monument in Spain, attracting millions of visitors annually to its intricate Nasrid palaces.
