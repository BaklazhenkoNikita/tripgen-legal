Auckland Travel Guide

Known as the 'City of Sails,' Auckland is a vibrant, multicultural metropolis uniquely situated on a narrow isthmus between two major harbors. It seamlessly blends stunning volcanic landscapes, world-class waterfront dining, and a deep-rooted connection to both Māori heritage and Pacific Island culture.

About Auckland

The Auckland isthmus has been inhabited by Māori since approximately 1350, valued for its fertile volcanic soil and strategic position, which led to the construction of numerous fortified villages known as pā. Following the signing of the Treaty of Waitangi, the city was officially founded in 1840 by British settlers and served as the capital of New Zealand from 1842 until 1865, when the seat of government was moved to Wellington. Throughout the 20th century, Auckland evolved from a colonial outpost into a diverse, cosmopolitan hub. Today, it stands as the economic engine of New Zealand, having grown significantly through waves of migration that have shaped its modern, multicultural identity while maintaining respect for its indigenous history.

Auckland’s culture is a dynamic fusion of European, Māori, and Pacific Island influences, making it the largest Polynesian city in the world. This diversity is celebrated through vibrant events like the Pasifika Festival, which showcases traditional music, dance, and crafts, as well as a thriving culinary scene that highlights fresh local seafood and Pacific-inspired flavors. Locals, often called 'Aucklanders,' embrace a laid-back, outdoor-oriented lifestyle. Whether it is sailing on the Waitematā Harbour, hiking up dormant volcanic cones, or exploring the bustling weekend markets, the city’s culture is defined by its proximity to nature and a welcoming, inclusive spirit.

Quick facts

  • Auckland is built on a volcanic field containing more than 50 dormant volcanoes.
  • It is one of the few cities in the world to have harbors on two separate major bodies of water: the Pacific Ocean and the Tasman Sea.
  • The city has the highest boat ownership per capita in the world, earning it the nickname 'City of Sails.'
  • You can walk from the Pacific Ocean to the Tasman Sea on foot via the 16-kilometer Coast to Coast Walkway.
  • Auckland is home to the largest ethnic Polynesian population in the world.
