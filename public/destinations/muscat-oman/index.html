Muscat Travel Guide

Muscat is a captivating coastal capital where rugged Hajar Mountains meet the turquoise waters of the Gulf of Oman, offering a unique blend of ancient tradition and modern refinement. Unlike many of its neighbors, the city eschews towering skyscrapers in favor of low-rise, white-washed architecture that preserves its authentic, historic charm.

About Muscat

Muscat has served as a vital maritime trading hub since the 1st century CE, drawing merchants from India, Persia, and Africa due to its deep natural harbors. Its strategic importance led to centuries of power struggles, most notably a period of Portuguese occupation from 1507 to 1650, which left behind the iconic Al Jalali and Al Mirani forts that still guard the harbor today. In the 18th century, Muscat became the center of a powerful maritime empire that extended its influence as far as Zanzibar. Following the accession of Sultan Qaboos bin Said in 1970, the city underwent a rapid, thoughtful modernization process that transformed it into the vibrant, culturally rich administrative and economic heart of the Sultanate it is today.

Omani culture is deeply rooted in Ibadi Islam and the values of hospitality, modesty, and respect. Traditional life is still very much alive in the bustling Muttrah Souq, where the air is thick with the scent of frankincense and the sounds of merchants trading in silver, textiles, and spices. The city is also a burgeoning hub for the arts, exemplified by the Royal Opera House Muscat, which serves as a stunning bridge between traditional Islamic design and world-class performing arts. Omani cuisine is a flavorful reflection of its history, featuring dishes like 'shuwa' (slow-cooked spiced lamb) and 'majboos' (aromatic rice with meat), often enjoyed with a cup of cardamom-spiced coffee.

Quick facts

  • Muscat is one of the few capitals in the world that strictly regulates building heights to maintain a traditional, low-rise aesthetic.
  • The Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque houses one of the world's largest hand-woven Persian carpets, which took 600 women four years to complete.
  • Oman is famous for its high-quality frankincense, which has been traded through Muscat's ports for thousands of years.
  • The city is home to the Royal Opera House, the first of its kind in the Persian Gulf region, reflecting the late Sultan's passion for classical music and arts.
  • Muscat's natural harbors were so strategically significant that they were described by ancient geographers as 'hidden harbors' due to their protective rocky surroundings.
