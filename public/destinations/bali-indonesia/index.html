Bali Travel Guide

Bali is a mesmerizing Indonesian island renowned for its lush volcanic mountains, iconic terraced rice paddies, and vibrant coral reefs. Often called the 'Island of the Gods,' it offers a unique blend of profound spiritual heritage, world-class surfing, and a sophisticated, bohemian lifestyle.

About Bali

Bali's history is deeply intertwined with the influence of Indian, Chinese, and particularly Hindu culture, which arrived via the Majapahit Empire in the 14th century. When Islam spread across Java, many intellectuals, artists, and priests fled to Bali, creating a unique 'Hindu-Balinese' enclave that has preserved its distinct religious and cultural identity for centuries. In the early 20th century, the island faced Dutch colonial intervention, culminating in the tragic 'Puputan' (ritual mass suicides) as local royalty resisted colonial rule. Following Indonesia's independence in 1945, Bali evolved from a quiet agricultural society into a global tourism powerhouse, while remarkably maintaining its traditional village structures and religious practices.

Balinese culture is defined by _Tri Hita Karana_, a philosophy emphasizing harmony between humans, nature, and the divine. Daily life is punctuated by the offering of _canang sari_—small, colorful woven baskets filled with flowers and incense—placed on sidewalks and shrines to appease spirits. The arts are central to life here, with intricate woodcarving, traditional gamelan music, and mesmerizing dance performances like the Kecak fire dance being integral parts of temple ceremonies. The food scene is a delicious exploration of spice and texture, featuring staples like _nasi goreng_ (fried rice) and _babi guling_ (spit-roasted suckling pig). Dining in Bali ranges from humble, roadside _warungs_ serving authentic local fare to high-end, internationally acclaimed restaurants that utilize the island's abundant tropical produce and fresh seafood.

Quick facts

  • Bali has its own unique calendar system called the Pawukon, which consists of only 210 days in a year.
  • The island is home to the world's most expensive coffee, Kopi Luwak, which is made from beans digested by the Asian palm civet.
  • Nyepi, the Balinese Day of Silence, requires the entire island to shut down—no lights, no travel, and no work—to trick evil spirits into thinking the island is uninhabited.
  • Bali is the only province in Indonesia where the majority of the population practices Hinduism, rather than Islam.
  • The island is part of the Coral Triangle, which holds the highest marine biodiversity on Earth.
