Munich Travel Guide

Munich is a vibrant Bavarian capital that perfectly balances centuries-old traditions with a modern, cosmopolitan energy. Renowned for its world-famous beer culture, stunning architecture, and sprawling green spaces, it offers a unique blend of historical charm and high-tech innovation.

About Munich

Munich was founded in 1158 by Duke Henry the Lion, who established a market and a bridge over the River Isar, with its name derived from the Old High German word for 'by the monks.' For over 700 years, the city was the seat of the powerful Wittelsbach dynasty, which transformed it into a magnificent residence city filled with palaces and grand public buildings. In the 19th century, under the patronage of King Ludwig I and King Ludwig II, Munich blossomed into a major European center for art, science, and music. Despite suffering severe destruction during World War II, the city was meticulously restored and has since evolved into a thriving, prosperous hub of technology, finance, and culture.

Bavarian culture is the heartbeat of Munich, defined by the concept of 'Gemütlichkeit'—a unique sense of warmth, friendliness, and belonging. This is best experienced in the city's legendary beer gardens, where locals and visitors alike gather to enjoy hearty classics like Weisswurst, Schweinshaxe, and fresh pretzels in a relaxed, communal atmosphere. The city is also a powerhouse of arts and innovation, boasting world-class museums like the Pinakotheken and a deep-rooted appreciation for classical music and opera. Whether it is the traditional Tracht (Lederhosen and Dirndls) worn during festivals or the cutting-edge design found in its modern neighborhoods, Munich celebrates its heritage while embracing a forward-thinking, creative spirit.

Quick facts

  • The world-famous Oktoberfest actually began in 1810 as a royal wedding celebration, not as a beer festival.
  • Munich is home to a permanent river surfing spot on the Eisbach wave, where surfers ride the current year-round.
  • The city's famous Glockenspiel in the New Town Hall performs a mechanical show twice daily, reenacting historical events with life-sized figures.
  • Munich has an official 'beer garden season' that typically runs from mid-April through October.
  • The city's name literally translates to 'Home of the Monks,' which is why a monk appears on the city's coat of arms.
