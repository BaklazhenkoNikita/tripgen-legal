Hong Kong Travel Guide

Hong Kong is a dazzling, high-energy metropolis where futuristic skyscrapers collide with ancient traditions and lush, mountainous landscapes. Known as the 'Fragrant Harbour,' this Special Administrative Region offers a unique, fast-paced fusion of East and West that is unlike anywhere else on Earth.

About Hong Kong

Originally a collection of quiet fishing villages, Hong Kong was ceded to Britain following the First Opium War in the 19th century, transforming into a vital global trading port. Over the next 150 years, it evolved into a world-class financial hub, characterized by its resilience and rapid modernization. In 1997, sovereignty was transferred back to China under the 'One Country, Two Systems' framework. This historic handover preserved Hong Kong's distinct legal, economic, and social systems, allowing it to maintain its unique identity as a global gateway while integrating into the broader Chinese landscape.

Hong Kong's culture is a vibrant kaleidoscope where Cantonese heritage meets colonial history and modern global influence. You will find locals practicing ancient _Feng Shui_ in ultra-modern offices, while traditional festivals like the Cheung Chau Bun Festival and the Mid-Autumn Festival are celebrated with as much fervor as international art fairs. The food scene is legendary, ranging from humble, bustling _dai pai dong_ (open-air food stalls) serving iconic dim sum and roast goose to Michelin-starred fine dining. It is a city that never sleeps, where the rhythm of life is set by the constant hum of the Star Ferry, the clatter of trams, and the relentless energy of its street markets.

Quick facts

  • Hong Kong flushes its toilets using seawater to conserve fresh water resources.
  • The city is surprisingly green, with over 40% of its land area designated as country parks and nature reserves.
  • Due to the cultural belief that the number four sounds like the word for 'death,' many buildings omit the fourth floor entirely.
  • Hong Kong is an archipelago consisting of 263 islands, not just the main urban island.
  • It holds the title for having more skyscrapers than any other city in the world.
