Kotor Travel Guide

Nestled at the foot of dramatic limestone cliffs along a secluded, fjord-like bay, Kotor is a breathtaking medieval gem known for its labyrinthine streets and ancient fortifications. This UNESCO World Heritage site perfectly blends Venetian-era architecture with the stunning natural beauty of the Adriatic coast.

About Kotor

Founded as the Roman settlement of Acruvium, Kotor has served as a vital maritime and commercial hub for centuries, passing through the hands of Byzantium, medieval Serbia, and the Venetian Republic. Its strategic location made it a coveted prize, resulting in the construction of its formidable 4.5-kilometer-long city walls that climb the steep mountainside to protect the town from invaders. Throughout its history, Kotor has faced numerous challenges, including devastating earthquakes, most notably in 1979. This disaster caused significant damage to its historic structures, but a massive international restoration effort, supported by UNESCO, successfully preserved the town's unique Romanesque, Gothic, and Baroque character for future generations.

Kotor’s culture is deeply rooted in its maritime heritage and a vibrant, multi-layered history that celebrates everything from religious festivals to modern arts. The town is famous for its lively squares, where locals and visitors alike gather to enjoy the Mediterranean food scene, which features fresh Adriatic seafood, local olive oil, and traditional Balkan delicacies like _pršut_ (cured ham) and local cheeses. Beyond its culinary delights, the city is known for its cheerful, welcoming spirit and a unique local affection for its feline population. Cats are considered the unofficial guardians of the city, and you will find them lounging in historic squares, celebrated in local shops, and even honored with their own museum and dedicated spaces throughout the Old Town.

Quick facts

  • Kotor is often called the 'Bride of the Adriatic' due to its stunning beauty and dramatic bay setting.
  • The winding, confusing layout of the Old Town's streets was intentionally designed to disorient and trap invading forces.
  • Cats are so revered in Kotor that they have become a symbol of the city, with a dedicated museum and numerous cat-themed shops.
  • The Bay of Kotor is technically a submerged river canyon, often referred to as Europe's southernmost fjord.
  • The city walls are so extensive that they stretch 4.5 kilometers and reach heights of up to 20 meters.
